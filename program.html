The program

The series, run the way a trading firm runs a trainee class: a short track of ideas in order, a pit where you answer against a clock, and a ledger that decides what you practice next. The pages show you a thing happening. This page finds out whether you can do it.

  1. The track — seven stations. Each one names a page to watch, drills to clear, and one thing to do on a real screen.
  2. The pit — forty timed drills with computed answers, and a ten-rep head-math sprint. Some screens have something to trade. Some have nothing, and saying so is the right answer.
  3. The ledger — every rep is graded and scheduled to come back. It also prints a one-page readiness report.
  4. The edge map — where option edge comes from, one family at a time, and what kills each.
  5. The trade memo — the blanks to fill before any trade, printable.

04

Where option edge comes from, and what kills each kind

A map of where to look. It is not evidence that the edge is there today.

Every trade worth doing sits in one of these families. Before putting one on, name the family, the signal and its number, the structure that isolates it, who is on the other side, and what kills it. If one of those is blank, the trade is not ready.

1 · Carry and financing
Mispriced
The interest rate, dividend or borrow implied by call minus put.
Signal
The rate a conversion, box or jelly roll locks in, against your own funding.
Structure
Conversion, reversal, box, jelly roll.
Other side
Someone paying for financing or for a hard-to-borrow short through the options.
What kills it
A dividend change, early assignment, a pin, a borrow recall. A low implied rate is usually the borrow fee, not cheap money. The edge is cents, so costs decide it.
Drill
2 · Exercise and corporate actions
Mispriced
Mostly your own leakage: value given away by a wrong exercise, tender or adjustment call.
Signal
Exercise a call the night before the ex-date only if the dividend beats the interest on the strike plus the put you throw away.
Structure
The exercise decision itself, and cleaning up near-the-money positions before expiry.
Other side
Nobody pays you. You stop paying others — and holders who fail to exercise hand the dividend to whoever is short.
What kills it
Assignment is random, so hedge to the expectation and size for the spread. Pin risk dwarfs the intrinsic at stake. The exercise chain itself can fail.

3 · Vol level
Mispriced
The whole level of implied vol against what the stock will realize.
Signal
A clean base vol plus events, compared with the market. A month of realized vol is a noisy estimate.
Structure
Straddle or variance, delta-hedged.
Other side
Short: buyers of insurance. Long: overwriters and put sellers pressing vol down.
What kills it
Path dependence — the moves land where your gamma is. Hedging error. Jumps. P&L that arrives in lumps.
Drill
4 · Term structure and events
Mispriced
One expiry out of line with its neighbors, or an event move priced wrong.
Signal
Forward vol between expiries; the event move backed out of two expiries.
Structure
A calendar, sized by root-time vega; front against back across the event.
Other side
Hedgers and lottery buyers in the front expiry.
What kills it
A calendar that is flat in raw vega is short the front. Short-dated vol moves more. Fat tails.
Drill
5 · Skew and curvature
Mispriced
One tail against the other, or the wings against the middle.
Signal
The risk reversal and the fly against their own history.
Structure
Risk reversal, delta-hedged; fly, or strangle against straddle.
Other side
Buyers of crash insurance; overwriters on the call side.
What kills it
Your delta depends on how you assume the smile moves. A regime change makes history a trap.

6 · Correlation
Mispriced
Index vol against the vols of its components.
Signal
Implied correlation. Above one means a bad mark.
Structure
Short index variance, long single-name variance.
Other side
Index hedgers.
What kills it
Correlation goes to one in a crash while single-name vols rise. It often loses on the first leg down.
Drill
7 · Flow and relative value
Mispriced
A name cheap or rich to its peers and its own history because of who is trading it.
Signal
Where its vol sits in its own cone, and who the flow is.
Structure
Buy the cheap vol, sell the rich, hedge the direction.
Other side
The flow itself: overwriting programs, structured products, put sellers.
What kills it
Cheap that is only a flow artifact. Crowding. A premium that lasted years and then reversed.

8 · Providing liquidity
Mispriced
Nothing. You are paid the spread for standing there.
Signal
Where the price goes after your fills.
Structure
Quote the edge in vol points, hedge the delta, manage the rest by leaning your vols.
Other side
Anyone who needs to trade now.
What kills it
Adverse selection: the fills you get are the ones you would rather not have.
Read
Microstructure
9 · Broken bounds
Mispriced
A butterfly priced below zero, or total variance that falls with expiry.
Signal
The sign of the cost, checked before any Greek.
Structure
Sell the body and buy the wings; sell the near expiry and buy the far.
Other side
Usually nobody: a stale or wide quote.
What kills it
It does not trade where it is marked. Check the quote is live before believing it.

If you cannot fill in the blanks, you do not have a trade

One page, written before the trade, that someone else can read in a minute.

Drills price with Black–Scholes, European exercise and the carry each one states; real chains are American, have dividends and trade at a bid and an offer. Grades come from this page, not from a person: they show whether a calculation is reliable under a clock, not whether a trade is good. The ledger lives in this browser's storage and nowhere else.
