Two Rulers

Standard deviation, mean absolute deviation, and the 25% gap between them

An option pays an absolute value, so a straddle prices the expected absolute move — and the screen quotes a standard deviation beside it. For a normal the two differ by √(2/π) = 0.7979, which is why the implied move is breached 42.5% of the time rather than the 31.7% a one-sigma reading expects. Then the part that constant hides: 0.7979 belongs to the normal alone, falling to exactly 3/4 at six degrees of freedom and 1/√2 at four, so the same straddle implies a bigger sigma the fatter the tails. Ends on the same choice made on realized data, where one estimator is steady and the other is honest.
